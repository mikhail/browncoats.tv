Prototype thesis

Don’t let “too soon” happen again. Don’t use a landlord.

Firefly has a fanbase that has already proven long-term loyalty. A self-hosted official home would let the team speak to that audience directly instead of depending entirely on another company’s platform, algorithm, and priorities.

Why self-host

Use platforms for reach. Keep the official home under Firefly’s control.

Platform distribution can reach viewers, but it often keeps the customer relationship, the merchandising path, the watch data, and the programming surface. A direct fan hub gives the Firefly team an owned front door — whether the series ultimately lives on a platform, goes direct, or both.

📊

First-party fan relationship

Announcements, accounts, events, merchandise paths, and community touchpoints can belong to the official team — not disappear inside a platform dashboard.

▶

Programming control

Premieres, watch parties, aftershows, and creator chats can be scheduled around fan rituals instead of platform algorithms.

⚖

Stronger negotiating position

An owned destination gives the team more leverage before any broader streaming, licensing, or distribution deal.

Platform economics

Use platforms for reach. Preserve the first-party fan relationship.

Every distribution channel involves trade-offs. Understanding the economics helps the Firefly team decide where to syndicate, where to go direct, and how to keep an owned fan relationship.

YouTube

Watch-page ads offer roughly 55% creator revenue share. Memberships and fan funding net around 70%. But the platform owns the audience relationship and algorithm-driven discovery.

Good for reach and discovery. Less control over audience data and programming surface.

Amazon Prime Video Direct

Selected/licensed content at Amazon’s discretion. Prime payout rate-card can be as low as roughly $0.01–$0.12/hour in the US. TVOD splits around 50%. Payment lag can be significant.

Massive catalog reach. Revenue per-viewer and audience ownership are limited.

Netflix

Closed licensing/acquisition model. Not a self-serve platform — content is selected, not uploaded. No direct fan relationship or self-serve revenue channel.

Premium exposure if selected. No direct fan relationship or self-serve official home.

Vimeo OTT / white-label

Direct-to-consumer platform economics: typically around $1/subscriber/month plus merchant fees. Useful as a white-label vendor route for running a branded fan hub.

One possible vendor for a Firefly-owned hub. Not the only option — the team would choose their own stack.

Precedent: Dropout

Direct-to-fan for creative freedom and reliable audience economics.

Dropout (formerly CollegeHumor) moved to a direct subscription model for creative independence and more predictable audience economics. Their experience shows that a dedicated fanbase can sustain a direct platform when the content and community are strong enough. Firefly’s proven multi-decade fandom suggests that an official home is worth considering alongside any platform-first strategy.

Dropout’s context differs from Firefly’s. This is a directional precedent, not a 1:1 comparison.
